-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1.  HOW WE WORK TOGETHER  (the contract)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These are YOUR rules, written down so they survive across sessions:

  - You are learning to DESIGN and BUILD databases (and eventually the app)
    yourself. The goal is your understanding and judgement, not finished code.

  - Biggest picture first, then major -> minor - and that same descent again
    INSIDE each phase. Before any piece: what is it, how does it work, what could
    or could not be done with it, and should it exist at all.

  - What and why BEFORE how. We never jump to commands/steps before you
    understand the thing and have had room to think, question, and decide.

  - I do NOT modify your project for you. I teach and explain; when you are
    ready, YOU do it in student-panel-I; you report what you did; we continue
    from there.

  - We COMPARE your choices against the reference build and surface every place
    you would design differently - not to correct you, but so both of us can see
    the decision clearly. Different is often fine.

  - Pace is yours. "Don't rush, take as long as you need." We can stop, loop
    back, or go deeper anytime.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  2.  THE PHASE SCHEDULE  (adjustable - tell me what to change)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Note the deliberate ordering: INSTALLATION IS LAST. You asked to treat setup as
"for the end", so we learn the whole design first and only stand it up on a real
machine once it will mean something.

  PHASE 0 - ORIENTATION  (mostly reading; you are here)
     Read the database lesson. Do the Part 7 contemplations, especially the
     blank-page design. Agree on this roadmap (edit it). No tools, no building.

  PHASE 0.5 - DESIGN FOUNDATIONS  (the general craft, before student-panel specifics)
     Added at your request ("I need to first understand ER designing... learn
     designing first. it is my first time."). Read database-design-primer.txt end
     to end - it teaches how to DESIGN any database from a blank page (entities,
     attributes, relationships, cardinality, keys, ER diagrams, the hard patterns,
     normalisation, and a repeatable 10-step method), using small fresh examples,
     not student-panel. Do its Part 10 practice on paper. Then we re-derive the
     student-panel schema together using that same method - which flows straight
     into Phase 1 and your read-through questions.

  PHASE 1 - THE BUILDING BLOCKS + YOUR BLANK-PAGE DESIGN
     Work through the ~11 core ideas (Part 2 of the lesson) until they are
     yours: keys, foreign keys, relationship shapes, null, constraints, indexes,
     soft-delete, jsonb, derive-don't-store, migrations. Then lay YOUR sketch
     beside the reference and study the differences together.
  PHASE 2 - THE BIG PICTURE: FACTS AND DIMENSIONS
     The heart of the schema: evaluations is a FACT; students/groups,
     parameters, periods are its DIMENSIONS. We make sure you can look at the ER
     diagram and narrate the whole system from the middle outward, in your own
     words, before touching any table detail.

  PHASE 3 - DESIGN THE DIMENSIONS YOURSELF  (first hands-on, in student-panel-I)
     You design and create the dimension tables one at a time: students (+ the
     custom-fields idea), the student_groups TREE and its membership bridge,
     the parameters TREE, and periods. For each: we contemplate it first, you
     write it in student-panel-I, you report, we compare to the reference.

  PHASE 4 - THE CORE: EVALUATIONS
     The richest table. Nullability (group required, student optional and what
     that means), the group-or-student XOR, the two partial-unique "one score
     per who/what/when" rules, value validation living in the app, and locking.
     You design it, build it, and we compare carefully.

  PHASE 5 - ASSIGNMENTS, TASKS, AND DERIVED VALUES
     The three XOR assignment tables (parameter/period/task) and the tasks pair;
     then the "derive, don't store" machinery - averaging leaf parameters into a
     parent at read time - and the tree-walking queries (Part 5 walkthroughs).

  PHASE 6 - MIGRATIONS & STORAGE REALITY
     How the schema is actually built and evolved: forward-only migrations,
     schema_migrations bookkeeping, transactions; and the PGlite-vs-PostgreSQL
     story plus the adapter seam. You write and run your own migration.

  PHASE 7 - INSTALLATION & RUNNING IT FOR REAL  (LAST, as you asked)
     Treat it as a fresh Linux device, end to end: prerequisites, getting the
     schema created, seeding, and running - the "how" that only now, at the end,
     is worth its time because you understand every piece it stands up.

  (Every phase runs the same major->minor, contemplate-first loop from section 3.
   Phases can split or merge as we go.)


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  3.  THE PER-PHASE LOOP
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Inside every phase we repeat:

     (a) CONTEMPLATE - I pose what/why/could/should questions; you think first.
     (b) TEACH       - I explain the piece, big picture then details.
     (c) YOU DO      - when you are ready, you build that piece in student-panel-I.
     (d) REPORT      - you tell me what you did and what puzzled you.
     (e) COMPARE     - we lay your version beside the reference and name the
                       differences and the reasons.
     (f) ADJUST      - we update this roadmap and the progress log, then continue.
